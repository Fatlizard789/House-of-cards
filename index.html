The House of Trading Cards

The rise of trading cards in the modern era is a wonder that begs the question.
Where did trading cards come from? How is it so popular? How is it so valuable?

The Market

Trading Cards Market, A industry driven by passion and greed.

Trading cards is a very unique part of the entertainment industry. Because although cards like pokemon, magic the gathering and one peice to name a few have a purpose through the TCG (Trading Card Game) with every card with printed actions and interactions. Many people choose to collect them for the sole purpose of having them. And it isn't the same as other collection hobbies such as coins, books/comics, and watches/jewelry. Where they have value either by the knowledge they provide or the gold and metal in thier construction, trading cards are essentially just flashy cardboard.

But why are they so valued that people prefer to trade cards than stocks? Trading cards make money off of people's childhood with many cards having pictures/artwork of thier favorite people or creature and in many cases the trading cards themselves are apart of thier childhoods. It gives people a sense of attachment that other things cannot replicate. This gives the cards a already existing fanbase along with their wallets.

Scarcity and Greed

120,660 burgers , 20 cars and 2 houses or a single card?

A Black Lotus (1993) magic the gathering card sold for 3 million dollars USD on April 29, 2024 and a year later a Upper Deck Exquisite Dual Logoman card sold for 4 times that amount. In 2020 the covid-19 pandemic hit the world like a storm throwing people behind closed doors. People were out of work, denied access to the outside and growing bored and 90s Nostalgia, hit with many kids born in the 90s becoming adults. This was the perfect storm for trading cards, Media and internet influencers spread the hobby like wildfire with cards on ebay skyrocketing in price. This also exploded the reselling market people who sold packs of cards or sold singles were making thousands of dollars overnight. This caused scalpers who would turn thier career into becoming assholes buying all the stock and forcing hobbyists to pay thier prices.

Production Limits

Why doesn't the industry just mass produce to drop the prices?

Making trading cards arent as simple as cutting cardboard and printing a image. They are made through a complex process that gives it the high quailty shine that is unique to the trading card world. In addition there are only a handful of specialized, high-security printing facilities in the world that are capable of producing these cards. With hundreds of different companies fighting over securing production schedules in these facilities it creates supply shortages. And the publishers only print orders that are placed by distributor and retailers that were made months before so they cannot simply print more if the demand is higher than expected they would have make another order that could take 6 - 12 months.
