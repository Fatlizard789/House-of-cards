Origins

In the mid 19th century, cigarettes were sold in poorly made paper or cloth packs that would get crushed in peoples pockets to fix this issue the tobacco companies added a piece of stiff cardboard to reinforce the packet. By the late 1870s the companies realized that these peices of cardboard could be used for advertising so they printed images on them to incourage brand loyalty if a person wanted the complete set they needed to buy the same brand of cigarettes.

Pictures and Art

From the start companies wanted to design the cards to appeal to men.

  • Actresses and Opera Stars: The earliest popular subjects were famous performers of the era.
  • Military Heroes & Flags: Highlighting historical figures, generals, and global themes.
  • Nature and Animals: Beautifully colored wildlife illustration series.
  • Early Sports: In 1887, Goodwin & Company produced the famous Old Judge and Gypsy Queen cigarette brands, which featured the very first continuous run of photographic baseball cards, capturing players in artificial "action" poses inside a photography studio.

Between 1909 and 1911, the American Tobacco Company (ATC) used its monopoly power to launch the historic T206 card set, featuring 524 baseball player lithographs packed inside cigarette brands like Sweet Caporal and Piedmont to crush rival brands and possibly the most influential card ever. What makes this set so historic is the Honus Wagner card, Honus Wagner one of the first five charter members of the Baseball Hall of Fame denied use of his likeness shortly after production making it a very popular and scarce card. The T206 Honus Wagner remains the undisputed blueprint for how artificial scarcity, historical preservation, and a great origin story can turn a tiny piece of packaging insert into a multi-million-dollar relic.
